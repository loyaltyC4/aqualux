How important is spectrum versus intensity?

Both light spectrum and intensity are critical for vigorous plant growth. Plants require a balanced spectrum that includes specific blue and red wavelengths to fuel photosynthesis efficiently. Blue light (around 400-500nm) primarily drives chlorophyll production and compact, bushy growth, while red light (around 600-700nm) promotes stem elongation, flowering, and overall biomass accumulation. Without both, plants can become leggy, pale, or fail to thrive.

48 cm model supplies white and blue LEDs, which are effective for low‑to‑moderate growth and suitable for many common plant species. The RGBW 55 improves on this by adding red wavelengths through its RGBW mix, giving a broader spectrum that better supports diverse plant needs at a lower wattage. The Aurora Smart 78 combines 5054 and 5730 diodes, delivering a comprehensive full spectrum that covers the critical blue‑red range while still putting out a high lumen count. This combination ensures plants receive all the necessary light energy for rapid, healthy development.

Intensity, measured in lumens, matters significantly, especially in densely planted tanks or those with a taller water column. Light diminishes as it travels through water. A stronger light source pushes more light deeper into the tank, ensuring that even low‑lying carpeting plants or the lower leaves of taller stem plants receive adequate illumination. The 34 W output of the Smart 78 is powerful enough to penetrate deeper water, reducing the need for additional fixtures to properly illuminate all areas of a larger tank.

In contrast, the 20 W Aurora 48 or 15 W RGBW 55, while effective for shallower or smaller tanks, may leave the back of a 75 cm tank under‑lit, especially if you use taller plants like Amazon swords. In such cases, the lower light intensity can become a limiting factor for growth. You can still achieve good growth by pairing a lower‑intensity light with a CO₂ system and proper fertilization, but the plants will not grow as fast or as densely as they would under a higher‑intensity light like the Smart 78, even with CO₂ present. The goal is to provide enough light that it doesn't hold back the other growth factors.

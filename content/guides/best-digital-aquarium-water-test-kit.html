Common mistakes and cheap alternatives to skip

Buying a single‑parameter pH pen and hoping it will tell you about hardness is a mistake. Hardness lives in the conductivity reading. Skipping calibration because it feels tedious will ruin accuracy. Some sellers push a "budget" meter that lacks a proper probe; those units are fragile and give noisy data. Stick with a reputable brand that includes a proper probe and buffer solution.

How to maintain and calibrate a digital meter

Calibration is the single most important step. For pH meters, use the two‑point buffer powder supplied with the unit. First, dip the probe in a neutral buffer, set the display, then repeat with a high‑pH buffer. Rinse the probe with distilled water between steps. Conductivity probes need a fresh conductivity solution; wipe the sensor clean after each use. Store the probe dry, and avoid leaving it submerged for long periods. Battery life is decent, the pH pen runs on two 1.5 V cells, the LCD meter uses a similar setup. Replace them when the display dims.

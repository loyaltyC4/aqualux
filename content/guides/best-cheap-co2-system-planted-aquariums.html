How to monitor and adjust CO2 output

First, set the regulator to a low working pressure. Watch the bubble counter. Aim for a steady stream of bubbles that matches the drop checker's colour change to the pink‑blue midpoint. If the colour stays deep blue, increase the pressure slightly. If it goes fully pink, back off.

Second, use a timer with the solenoid valve. A typical schedule runs CO2 from dawn until a few hours before the lights go off. This prevents excess CO2 buildup at night when plants are not photosynthesising.

Third, keep an eye on fish behaviour. Rapid surface breathing or lethargy signals too much CO2. Reduce the bubble rate or shorten the timer.

Pressurised cylinder vs. chemical generator: is the extra cost justified?

The pressurised cylinder route uses a standard CO2 bottle with a regulator. The advantage is a constant, controllable flow and the ability to refill at a local supplier. The downside is the upfront cost of the cylinder and the recurring cost of refills.

The chemical generator is cheaper to start and does not need a refillable bottle. It produces CO2 from a reaction that you can replenish with cheap ingredients. However, the output is less precise and can vary with temperature.

If you are only running a small tank and want to stay under $200 total, the generator combo is the clear winner. If you have a larger tank, need consistent dosing, and are willing to spend a bit more, the starter kit with a separate cylinder may be worth the investment.
